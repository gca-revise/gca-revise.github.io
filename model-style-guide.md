# Model paragraph style guide (from the teacher, 21 Oct)

Rules for writing the model paragraph that ends a set. Claude drafts, the teacher edits and approves; nothing goes live until `teacher_approved` is `yes` in `models.csv`.

## Level and voice
- Grade 8 to 9 content, but accessible: plain sentences, no showy words. Reach the high level with the key vocab and academic phrases students have been taught.
- British English spelling.
- About 250 to 330 words, one paragraph, **PETAL order (point, evidence, technique, analysis, link) but with no labels**.
- Names the act and scene. Never invents a quote or a fact about the play.

## Content rules
- Use one or two quotes, and **only quotes that are in the set** the paragraph ends. Use the techniques already labelled on those quotes where there are any. The build checks the set membership.
- Use some of the set's key vocab, correctly. Do not overuse it: it should sound natural, not like a word list. (Catalyst means something that speeds a change up without causing it.)
- Open with a point that sets a first impression against the real argument: "Initially, it seems that… However, upon closer inspection…" What seems true is about what happens (for example what the witches cause), not the thing the writer goes on to prove.
- Develop the analysis fully, not in a rush: say what the technique shows, what that suggests about the character, and why it matters. Where it fits, link to context (great chain of being, natural order, divine right to rule).
- Close with a thesis-style link, not "the writer wants the reader to realise": "Shakespeare wants to teach / warn / challenge the audience that…".

## Phrases to use (from the department's academic writing CPD and Lemov's *Art of the Sentence*)
- Extend sentences with because, but, so, therefore.
- "Initially, it seems… however, upon closer inspection…"; "Although… he ultimately…"; "Despite…"; "As a result of…"; "Furthermore"; "The [technique] suggests…".
- "X is a catalyst for…, driving him to…"; "Shakespeare warns us of the dangerous consequences of…"; "the chaos that ensues when…".
- Nominalisation (a verb turned into a noun) now and then: "the contrast between their reactions shows…"; "the psychological deterioration of…".

## Gap activity ("Complete the paragraph")
Blank the set's key vocab and the technique and academic words, not the quotes. Always write the gap list in `models.csv` (`gaps` column).
